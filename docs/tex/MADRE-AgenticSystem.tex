\section*{Abstract}
\section{Executive Definition}

\begin{pxQuote}
	\MADRE is a local-first, model-agnostic runtime kernel for building agentic systems that preserve user sovereignty while coordinating Complex Agents, module-local reasoning lanes, governed context, internal actions, memory, learning, recovery, and inspectable execution.
\end{pxQuote}
\madreLogoH*[21em] exists because the current AI market is normalizing a dangerous bargain: users receive convenient language interfaces, while their data, dependency chain, costs, and decision power move into remote platforms they do not control. That bargain is technically unnecessary and politically unacceptable for many individuals, teams, public-interest organizations, researchers, creators, and small developers.\n

The project does not treat a language model as a complete intelligence. A model is a powerful linguistic function: useful, fallible, statistically trained, expensive in context, and unsafe when it is allowed to become the whole system. \Madre puts that function inside an engineered runtime. The runtime decides what may be known, what may be sent, what may be stored, what may be acted on, and what must wait for stronger evidence or human authority.

The product objective is one coherent assistant experience backed by a durable kernel. The visible interaction can remain fast and conversational while module-local reasoning and reflection lanes decompose, schedule, check, audit, and resume deeper work. This is the practical meaning of Model Agnostic Delayed Reasoning Effort: useful local intelligence should come from architecture, context discipline, and bounded autonomy, not from blind dependence on one remote provider or one large model.\n

\Madre is therefore not a chatbot, not a prompt collection, not a wrapper around one vendor API, and not a task-management application. It is the runtime layer that applications use to coordinate models, internal actions, memory, knowledge, policies, and interfaces without surrendering the user's private domain to cloud-first AI infrastructure.

\section{Why \madreLogoH?}
\begin{itemize}
	\itembf Data Sovereignty:
	\subitem The first design obligation of \Madre is to keep the user's data under the user's control. Contemporary AI services often make private life, professional work, organizational knowledge, and creative material flow through remote systems by default. That default is not neutral. It concentrates power in the companies that own the infrastructure, the logs, the model services, the policy switches, and the billing relation.
	\subitem \Madre refuses to make surveillance a prerequisite for assistance. Local-first operation is not nostalgia and it is not a feature checkbox. It is a security boundary, a political boundary, and an engineering discipline. Private conversation, local knowledge, organizational memory, and sensitive work must remain local unless an explicit policy, classification, minimization, and audit path says otherwise.
	\subitem Internet access is treated as a risk boundary, not as a casual technical detail. Prompt injection, credential exposure, external action abuse, provider lock-in, and remote data retention are not exceptional edge cases. They are normal failure modes of cloud-first agentic systems. \Madre's architecture must make those failures harder by default and visible when they cannot be eliminated.
	\itembf Free Software Duty:
	\subitem \Madre is released under \texttt{\license} because source availability alone is not enough. The project aims to provide usable agentic infrastructure for people and organizations that cannot, or should not, depend on Big Tech platforms for every intelligent interface they build.
	\subitem The ethical argument is direct. The current generation of AI models was trained on an enormous inheritance of human language, labor, research, art, software, and social knowledge. Much of that inheritance was collected without meaningful consent, attribution, compensation, or democratic governance. A public-interest response cannot be limited to complaining about extraction while leaving all practical infrastructure inside the same corporate channels.
	\subitem \Madre's answer is to build an open framework that helps developers and creators run useful systems on ordinary machines, inspect how decisions are made, replace model runtimes and authorized integrations, and share improvements without asking permission from a platform owner. The goal is not to imitate proprietary AI products locally; the goal is to make a different kind of agentic infrastructure possible.
	\itembf
	Model Agnosticism:
	\subitem Model agnosticism is a technical requirement and an anti-lock-in principle. No single model, host, vendor, format, or prompt convention should define \Madre's architecture. Models improve, fail, disappear, become expensive, change license terms, and carry different bias and safety profiles. A serious runtime must survive that instability.
	\subitem \Madre treats model invocation as a governed internal action behind an explicit runtime boundary. The runtime must be able to choose light local models for low-effort work, stronger models for demanding reasoning, verification models for checks, embedding models for retrieval, and authorized runtime bindings for different model families. The kernel's job is to manage that variety without letting any model become the authority over policy, memory, actions, or truth.
	\subitem This posture also rejects AGI mythology as an engineering foundation. LLMs do not need to be treated as minds to be useful. They can be treated as language machines inside a disciplined system that supplies context, decomposes work, checks outputs, records evidence, and escalates uncertainty.
	\itembf Resource Discipline:
	\subitem Local-first AI will fail if it assumes everyone owns specialized hardware or can pay for unlimited remote inference. \Madre must be useful on ordinary commercial devices, and it must degrade intelligently when resources are scarce.
	\subitem The answer is not to pretend small models are magically equivalent to large ones. The answer is architecture: Complex Agent foreground lanes, reasoning and reflection lanes, task decomposition, caches, short-lived specialized agents, context minimization, repeated checks where they matter, and durable memory that avoids recomputing the same work. \Madre should spend compute like a scarce civic resource, not like an infinite billing stream.
	\subitem This is also an ecological position. Sending every minor task across the internet to high-consumption data centers is not a harmless default. A runtime that can do more locally, reuse knowledge, and postpone expensive reasoning until it is justified is a better engineering object and a more responsible social object.
	\itembf Knowledge Governance:
	\subitem Agentic systems become dangerous when they blur the boundary between text, knowledge, memory, instruction, evidence, and action. \Madre must make those boundaries explicit. A conversation turn is not a verified fact. A generated summary is not a source. An action result is not an instruction. A learned pattern is not automatically policy.
	\subitem Knowledge must be isolated by domain, provenance, sensitivity, and use. The user must be able to understand what knowledge is being used, correct it, detach it, delete it, or prevent it from crossing into another context. This is especially important for organizations and collectives that need their own reliable sources to resist the default bias of models trained on dominant social, commercial, and colonial narratives.
	\subitem \Madre should support learning, but only as governed evolution. Memories, derived knowledge, prompt improvements, routing rules, workflows, and model adaptation candidates must pass through quarantine, verification, promotion, and rollback boundaries. A system that learns without control is not autonomous in a useful sense; it is merely uncontrolled.
	\itembf Distributed Reasoning:
	\subitem \Madre's cognitive thesis is pragmatic. Human work does not happen as one monolithic response. We answer quickly, defer hard problems, retrieve memory, ask for help, test hypotheses, revise plans, and learn from outcomes. Agentic software can mirror that shape without pretending to reproduce the human mind.
	\subitem The foreground lane of a Complex Agent exists to preserve conversational flow and user attention. It should clarify, triage, summarize known local facts, and create reasoning work when an answer needs deeper analysis. Reasoning and reflection lanes can then plan, search, verify, invoke bounded internal actions, compare alternatives, and return results when there is enough evidence.
	\subitem The illusion of one coherent assistant should emerge from many controlled runtime actors. That illusion is useful for the user, but it must not hide the engineering truth: every agent, model invocation, action invocation, memory update, and learning proposal needs an accountable boundary. \Madre is designed so the assistant can feel unified without becoming opaque.
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Problem}

Modern language-model systems often combine private data, model calls, action execution, memory, and remote infrastructure inside opaque conversational interfaces. This makes useful demonstrations easy to produce, but it weakens local ownership, traceability, autonomy control, error recovery, and the ability to replace models or providers.

\Madre addresses this as a Software Engineering problem: how to design a local-first runtime in which language models remain fallible replaceable functions, while the software system owns policy, context, memory, internal actions, audit, recovery, and learning boundaries. The research problem is not to prove that models reason, but to define an architecture that extracts useful language behavior without surrendering system authority to model output.

\section{Expected Contributions}

\Madre's expected contribution is a reference architecture for local-first, model-agnostic delayed reasoning. The contribution combines Complex Agent foreground, reasoning, and reflection lanes, governed context bundles, explicit model invocation and internal action contracts, quarantined learning, and auditable recovery paths.

The novelty is not a new language model. It is the engineering arrangement that keeps language models useful while preventing them from becoming sources of truth, authority, policy, memory, or action. The architecture is intended to be useful for personal assistants, developer systems, organizational knowledge workers, domain systems, and local interfaces that require control over private knowledge.

\section{Novelty And Validation Strategy}

The first research claim is that a useful agentic system can be validated as a software architecture before it is validated as a broad automation product. \Madre's novelty is the conjunction of local-first execution, model-runtime and action boundaries, delayed reasoning, governed context, explicit policy outcomes, candidate-only learning, and audited recovery.

The validation strategy examines whether a local request can enter through bounded access surfaces, be triaged without unsupported claims, become durable delayed work when stronger evidence is needed, obtain a governed context bundle, cross a replaceable model runtime boundary, invoke a bounded internal action when policy allows it, produce an evidence-aware result candidate, expose status and audit evidence, and support correction, invalidation, supersession, rollback, or deletion/detachment.

The validation strategy treats negative paths as first-class evidence. Prompt or source injection must remain source data. Sensitive or unrelated material must be excluded from context. Model output must not become truth, policy, action authority, memory, or active knowledge by itself. Learning, prompt, routing, workflow, memory, and knowledge proposals remain inactive candidates unless a defined promotion boundary accepts them.

\section{Product Design}

\subsection{Product Scope}

\Madre provides a disciplined coordination layer underneath agentic applications. The kernel routes conversation, records delayed work, constructs bounded context, selects model invocation paths, controls bounded internal actions, manages candidate memory and knowledge, records audit evidence, and exposes recovery semantics.

The primary audience is developers and organizations building private, domain-aware, extensible assistant systems. Applications built on \Madre may vary in interface, domain, and policy, but they inherit the same authority model: the runtime kernel owns context, policy, action invocation, audit, and recovery boundaries.

\subsection{What \Madre Is}

\begin{itemize}
	\item A local-first runtime kernel for agentic systems.
	\item A model-agnostic coordination layer for multiple model families.
	\item A delayed-reasoning architecture that keeps conversation responsive while deeper work happens through module-local reasoning lanes.
	\item A policy-aware boundary for context, internal actions, memory, learning, and remote transfer.
	\item A developer foundation for building assistants, domain systems, and workflow-capable applications on top of governed local intelligence.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What \Madre Is Not}

\begin{itemize}
	\item \Madre is not a chatbot product. Chat is one possible surface over the kernel, not the kernel's definition.
	\item \Madre is not a prompt wrapper. Prompts are runtime inputs, not an architecture.
	\item \Madre is not a vendor abstraction layer whose purpose is merely to swap API providers.
	\item \Madre is not an unrestricted automation agent. Internal actions, external actions, memory mutation, and learning require explicit boundaries.
	\item \Madre is not a passive record of interaction history. The product is the governed runtime kernel and its architectural obligations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Core User Value}

\Madre lets an application answer quickly without pretending every answer can be safe, complete, or well-reasoned in the foreground. The Complex Agent foreground lane keeps the conversation alive: it can clarify intent, state known local facts, name uncertainty, and trigger deeper work. Module-local reasoning lanes perform the work that requires decomposition, evidence, internal actions, verification, or stronger context.

This separation gives users a better contract. They are not forced to choose between a quick hallucination and a slow black box. They get immediate conversation when that is appropriate, delayed reasoning when that is necessary, and inspectable boundaries when the system touches sensitive data or meaningful actions.

\subsection{Design Principles}

\begin{itemize}
	\itembf Local-first, not local-only: Local execution is the default because privacy, cost, resilience, and agency require a strong local base. Remote services may exist only as explicitly governed integrations with classification, minimization, policy, provenance, and audit.
	\itembf Model-agnostic by contract: Models are replaceable runtime functions. The kernel must not let any model provider define the system's truth model, policy boundary, memory format, or action authority.
	\itembf Foreground lane, durable reasoning: The Complex Agent foreground lane should preserve conversational flow. Expensive, uncertain, risky, or evidence-heavy work should become scheduled reasoning that can be resumed, inspected, verified, and delivered asynchronously.
	\itembf Context is a governed asset: Context is not just text stuffed into a prompt. It is selected, minimized, classified, sourced, scoped, and revoked according to the purpose of the task and the authority of the actor using it.
	\itembf Autonomy is bounded: \Madre must support unattended delayed work, but autonomy is legitimate only when the action is allowed, logged, recoverable for its risk level, and blocked from crossing stronger boundaries without authorization.
	\itembf Learning starts quarantined: Useful systems should improve from use, but learned candidates cannot quietly rewrite protected behavior. Memories, knowledge, prompts, routing preferences, and workflow improvements must be reviewable, promotable, reversible, and separable.
	\itembf Interfaces do not own the architecture:	The \texttt{madre} CLI, local read-first inspection console, graphical UI, speech surfaces, or domain applications are access surfaces. They must expose and operate the kernel without becoming the source of runtime authority.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Product Boundaries}

The product boundary covers runtime responsibilities and architecture obligations: local-first operation, model-agnostic invocation, Complex Agent lane coordination, governed context, bounded internal actions, memory and knowledge isolation, candidate learning, audit, and recovery.

\Madre may expose many application forms: personal assistants, research systems, developer environments, organizational knowledge workers, or domain-specific automation environments. Those applications may differ in interface and policy, but they must inherit the same kernel commitments: local-first operation, replaceable models, governed context, bounded internal actions, inspectable memory, quarantined learning, audit, and recovery.

\madreChapter{Operational Scenarios}

\section{Foreground Lane Continuity}

The foreground lane maintains the conversational thread, assigns or updates the current topic, asks for clarification when intent or risk is unclear, and answers only when the needed information is already available within the active context boundary. When a task needs research, verification, internal actions, broader context, or higher-effort reasoning, the foreground lane records the need as delayed work instead of forcing a premature answer.

\section{Delayed Research}

Research work begins when a Complex Agent identifies a question that cannot be answered safely from current local knowledge. The runtime checks whether sufficient local information exists, constructs a bounded reasoning request, and uses module-local reasoning execution to gather, compare, and verify evidence. Verified research results may become source material for governed knowledge extraction.

\section{Knowledge Extraction}

Knowledge extraction converts files, folders, reports, or other source material into auditable knowledge candidates. Extracted material remains separable from general memory until provenance, scope, sensitivity, usefulness, and correction rules are established. The user must be able to detach, correct, or delete a knowledge area without collapsing unrelated domains.

\section{Specialized Conversation Mutation}

When a conversation requires domain-specific expertise, the foreground lane may request a specialized conversational mode with a scoped context bundle and appropriate model configuration. The specialized session must remain named, trackable, and constrained by the same context and policy rules as delayed reasoning work.

\section{Workflow Creation}

\Madre is intended to support reusable workflows created through a user-facing contract rather than through hidden prompt chains. Workflow definitions may combine conditional logic, loops, prompts, internal action invocations, explicitly authorized external actions, model invocations, and other workflows, but each executable step remains a declared action subject to policy, audit, and recovery expectations.

\section{Developer Extension}

Developers should be able to add models, internal actions, authorized external actions, projections, context policies, workflows, interfaces, and non-deterministic analysis helpers without rewriting the kernel. Extension points must preserve \Madre's model-agnostic design: runtime authority remains in explicit contracts and policy boundaries, not in a specific provider, framework, or model family.

\section{High-Level Architecture}

\Madre is organized around a deterministic runtime kernel that coordinates governed reasoning modules through responsibility boundaries rather than through a fixed topology.

The central architectural rule is simple: the model is never the system. Models are invoked by the runtime under context, policy, internal action, memory, and audit constraints. Agents are runtime actors that combine a purpose, context boundary, model-selection strategy, action permissions, memory scope, and lifecycle. They may be short-lived, persistent, specialized, conservative, speculative, or workflow-bound, but they do not own the kernel. Model runtime bindings are infrastructure, not agents.

The kernel coordinates modules; it does not become the reasoning actor for every task. System-level reasoning belongs in governed modules. The MADRE core module handles incoming interaction, continuity, foreground-lane response, clarification, dynamic planning, delegation, and delayed-work decisions under kernel policy. Other reasoning modules handle bounded domains such as local knowledge, research, code writing, image generation, or resource optimization.

\subsection{Responsibility Layer Diagram}

Figure~\ref{fig:madre-responsibility-layers} presents the primary responsibility structure of \Madre. The diagram is not a sequential request pipeline. It describes the ownership boundaries that constrain all runtime behavior.

The \Madre runtime kernel is the deterministic system coordinator. It owns access-surface entry, module registration, module routing, scheduling, resource assignment, global policy, audit root, durable runtime state, and lifecycle boundaries. The kernel does not become the reasoning actor for ordinary tasks. It selects, activates, limits, and observes reasoning modules.

Reasoning modules are the runtime units that perform domain-bounded reasoning. The Core Module is one reasoning module among others, although it is the default first receiver for ordinary user interaction because it specializes in conversational continuity, clarification, general planning, and delegation. Other modules may specialize in research, knowledge extraction, code, media, resource optimization, or domain-specific work. A module may answer quickly, create delayed work, invoke internal actions, or reflect on its own outcomes when its local policy and assigned resources allow it.

Executable behavior is not exposed as arbitrary external tools. The normal execution path uses an Internal Action Registry composed of typed runtime-defined functions with declared inputs, outputs, side-effect class, policy requirements, audit evidence, and recovery expectations. The first reference implementation may implement these actions on a JVM runtime, but JVM binding is not an architecture-level authority. This registry is the canonical low-risk action mechanism. External services, host commands, provider-backed tools, remote model services, or arbitrary plugin surfaces are not part of the default path and require stronger authority boundaries.

The Local Model Runtime Boundary is below the module and action layer. It represents model profiles, decoding configuration, session/cache policy, device/resource policy, runtime bindings, and inference evidence. It is not a chatbot server and not an architectural authority. Generated model content returns to the module as candidate material subject to verification, audit, and promotion rules.


\subsection{Concurrent Agent Lanes}

\Madre separates immediate interaction from deeper reasoning, but this separation is not modeled as
two global subsystems. It is modeled as a recurrent execution pattern inside Complex Agents.

A Complex Agent may maintain three concurrent lanes. The foreground lane preserves conversational
continuity, answers only when safe known context is sufficient, asks for clarification when intent or risk
is unclear, and creates deferred work when more evidence is required. The reasoning lane performs
planning, decomposition, evidence gathering, action invocation, verification, and result construction
under module authority. The reflection lane reviews recent work, observes delayed outcomes, detects
failure patterns, and proposes inactive learning candidates.

This pattern may exist in any reasoning module. The Core Module uses it for general interaction and
delegation. A research module may use it for evidence comparison and verification. A knowledge module
may use it for extraction, provenance checks, correction, and deletion semantics. The kernel schedules
and constrains these lanes through module routing, resource limits, durable state, policy, and audit, but
the reasoning behavior itself belongs to module-local agents.

\section{Runtime Data Flow}

Runtime data flow in \Madre is concurrent, governed, and module-centered. A local request enters
through an access surface and is submitted to the kernel. The kernel records the request, applies the
relevant entry policy, and routes it to the Core Module by default or to another registered module when
routing evidence supports that decision.

Within the selected module, a Complex Agent may produce an immediate foreground response, create
or continue delayed reasoning work, invoke internal actions, request local model inference, and reflect on
recent outcomes. These activities are not independent architectural layers. They are coordinated lanes
inside module-local agents and constrained by module policy, kernel policy, resource assignment, durable
state, audit, and recovery rules.

The following subsections describe the canonical request narrative, the governed model/action
sequence, and the data-authority rules that prevent user input, source material, model output, action
results, or learning candidates from promoting themselves into trusted state.

\subsection{Canonical Request Narrative}

The canonical user request begins when an access surface submits a local request to the kernel. The
kernel does not interpret the request as trusted knowledge or policy. It records the event, applies entry
constraints, and routes the work to the Core Module unless another module is already selected by
session state, policy, or module registry evidence.

The receiving module evaluates the request through its module-local Complex Agent. If the foreground
lane has enough safe local context, it may produce an immediate answer. If intent, scope, or risk is
unclear, it asks for clarification. If the request requires evidence, stronger reasoning, internal actions,
model inference, or broader context, the reasoning lane creates or continues durable work. Reflection
may run after the response or after delayed outcomes to propose inactive learning, routing, prompt,
workflow, memory, or knowledge candidates.

Every significant step emits audit evidence. Context bundles, action invocations, model invocations,
candidate results, policy outcomes, failures, correction, rollback, invalidation, supersession, and
deletion/detachment remain inspectable through read-first surfaces.

\subsection{Governed Model And Action Sequence}

When module work requires executable behavior, the module invokes an internal typed action. The
default action mechanism is local, typed, runtime-defined, policy-gated, and auditable. It is not an open
plugin socket and not a network-exposed external action protocol.

A model invocation is one specialized internal action. Before invocation, the module supplies a governed
context bundle, a model strategy, and the applicable action policy. The input compiler prepares the
model-specific representation without allowing the model provider, template, or runtime format to
define \Madre's authority model. The decode controller applies the selected inference strategy, records
sampling and cache configuration, and returns generated material as a candidate.

Action results and model outputs are observations or candidates. They may inform the next reasoning
step, but they do not authorize actions, mutate memory, update trusted knowledge, change routing,
modify workflows, or promote learning. Promotion requires the appropriate authority boundary,
verification path, audit record, and recovery expectation.

\subsection{Data And Authority Rules}

\begin{itemize}
	\item User input enters as conversation, not as policy, memory, source truth, action authority, or model authority.
	\item Retrieved or extracted material enters with provenance, domain scope, sensitivity, confidence, and correction metadata.
	\item Context bundles are constructed, minimized, classified, scoped, and revocable before they reach an agent lane, internal action, or model invocation.
	\item Model output enters as generated candidate material that requires the appropriate level of verification before it can become an answer, record, action input, memory, knowledge, or learning candidate.
	\item Internal action results enter as bounded observations. They become trusted evidence only according to the action contract, verification path, and audit record.
	\item External action surfaces, host commands, remote services, arbitrary filesystem mutation, credentials, and irreversible effects require stronger authority boundaries than ordinary internal actions.
	\item Learning output enters quarantine first and cannot directly rewrite active runtime behavior.
	\end{itemize}

kernel coordination, reasoning modules, internal typed actions, local model runtime, audit, and recovery.

host-command execution, arbitrary filesystem mutation, network access, untrusted source acquisition,
or third-party integrations. These are not ordinary internal actions. They require classification,

to their risk class.

\subsection{Access Surfaces}

\Madre exposes access surfaces without allowing those surfaces to define the kernel. The
\texttt{madre} CLI submits local requests and exposes status or result evidence. A local read-first
inspection console may expose sessions, queued work, context bundles, policy outcomes, model
evidence, internal action evidence, candidates, audit, recovery, and runtime state. These surfaces
operate under the same policy and authority model as every other runtime interaction.

The inspection console is not a privileged mutation path. Its transport is intentionally not fixed at
architecture level. If a later implementation uses HTTP, sockets, embedded UI, or another mechanism,
that choice must be justified by deployment and security decisions and must preserve local-first,
read-first, policy-governed behavior.

Graphical, speech, product-specific, or integration surfaces remain access surfaces over the runtime rather
than privileged paths around it.

\madreChapter{Safety And Evolution Policy}

\section{Runtime Safety}

\Madre is intended to run useful delayed work while the user is not constantly
present. That requirement makes safety an architectural property, not a
documentation afterthought. The system must be able to explain what it did, why
it did it, what data it used, what boundary allowed it, and how the result can
be corrected or rolled back when the action is reversible.

\subsection{Policy Before Action}

The kernel must not expose executable behavior merely because a model can request it.
Internal actions, external calls, memory updates, knowledge promotion, workflow execution,
and learning changes need explicit policy before execution. The safe path should
be the normal path, and safe autonomous improvement should not require constant
user interruption. Unsafe paths should be technically isolated, auditable,
recoverable where possible, and visible to the responsible developer or
operator.

Typed runtime-defined internal actions represent the canonical bounded action
form. They may be allowed only through a policy-gated action boundary.
Remote action, host commands, arbitrary filesystem access, mutation actions,
provider-backed integrations, active learning promotion, and workflow mutation require
stronger authority boundaries than bounded internal action execution.

\section{Evolution Control}

\subsection{Evolution Rules}

\begin{itemize}
	\item Runtime learning is represented as a governed subsystem, not only as
	manual feedback collection.
	\item Learning strategies must declare what they are allowed to observe,
	propose, mutate, verify, and promote.
	\item Runtime learning cannot silently modify protected behavior.
	\item Learned candidates begin with source, evidence, scope,
	and rollback information before they are promoted.
	\item Knowledge promotion requires provenance, correction paths, and recovery
	options.
	\item Workflow and routing improvements may become default behavior through
	developer-defined checks, evaluation gates, and rollback policies, without
	requiring user authorization for every routine improvement.
	\item Model adaptation, fine-tuning, or weight updates are advanced
	model-evolution actions governed by stronger model-adaptation authority.
	\item Critical learning failures must be recoverable through snapshots,
	versioned state, VCS-backed records, or an equivalent rollback mechanism.
\end{itemize}

This policy keeps \Madre capable of evolving through conscious, checked learning
without reducing improvement to constant user authorization. The final user should be
interrupted as little as possible. Routine learning should be governed by
developer-defined strategies, evidence, tests, promotion gates, audit trails, and
rollback mechanisms; user authorization is reserved for boundaries where risk,
privacy, identity, external action, or policy explicitly requires it.

D-011 fixes the quarantine rule for memory, knowledge, prompt, routing,
workflow, and learning changes. Candidate creation is useful system evidence,
but it is not active promotion and it cannot silently rewrite protected
behavior.

\madreChapter{System Engineering Baseline}

\section{Engineering Problem}

\Madre addresses the engineering problem of coordinating language-model
model invocation without allowing the model to become the system. The runtime must
support foreground continuity, delayed reasoning, governed context, bounded internal actions,
memory, learning, audit, and recovery while preserving local ownership of data
and operational control.

The core difficulty is not only model accuracy. A useful local assistant must
decide when an answer is safe in the foreground lane, when work must be delayed,
which context may be exposed, which internal action may be invoked, which result
requires verification, and how a mistaken or unsafe outcome can be inspected,
corrected, or rolled back.

\section{Stakeholders And Concerns}

\Madre serves users who need private assistance, developers who need an
extensible runtime, organizations that need governed local knowledge, and
maintainers who must keep the system auditable and evolvable. Each stakeholder
depends on the same architectural guarantees: model independence, local-first
operation, explicit authority boundaries, understandable execution, and
recoverable state.

The principal concerns are privacy, cost, autonomy control, model substitution,
context minimization, data provenance, action safety, knowledge correction,
learning control, operational continuity, and evidence for system behavior. A
runtime that cannot expose those concerns as inspectable state cannot satisfy
\Madre's product objective, even if its conversational output appears fluent.

\section{Requirement Domains}

\Madre's functional baseline is organized around runtime domains. The foreground
lane domain preserves conversational flow, asks for clarification, and
creates deeper work when the answer requires evidence or internal actions. The delayed
reasoning domain records work durably, schedules it, verifies results, and
returns outcomes without erasing the foreground interaction.

The context domain selects, minimizes, classifies, scopes, and revokes
information before it reaches models or internal actions. The model runtime domain
invokes local or policy-authorized remote models behind replaceable contracts. The internal action
domain exposes declared side effects under policy and audit. The
knowledge and memory domain separates private memory, domain knowledge,
untrusted source material, and learned candidates.

The learning domain keeps improvement proposals quarantined until evidence,
evaluation, promotion, and rollback rules allow activation. The observability
domain exposes sessions, tasks, context bundles, model invocations, action invocations,
authorizations, failures, metrics, and recovery paths. The deployment domain keeps
ordinary local operation viable before optional remote integrations are
considered.

\section{Traceability Surface}

Each significant product claim in \Madre must be visible as system behavior. A
claim such as local-first operation corresponds to local execution paths,
storage boundaries, remote-transfer controls, and inspection evidence. A claim
such as model agnosticism corresponds to replaceable model profiles, runtime
contracts, and tests that prevent provider-specific assumptions from entering
the kernel.

The same trace applies to safety claims. Policy before action must appear as
an enforceable boundary around internal actions, external calls, memory mutation, workflow
execution, and learning promotion. Governed context must appear as provenance,
classification, minimization, scope, revocation, and audit. Delayed reasoning
must appear as durable work records, status transitions, verification, result
delivery, and recovery.

\section{Quality Attributes}

\Madre prioritizes data sovereignty, privacy, resource discipline,
responsiveness, reliability, recoverability, maintainability, portability,
auditability, and resistance to hallucination-driven action. These attributes
are not independent decorations; they define whether the runtime fulfills its
purpose.

Responsiveness is measured by preserving useful foreground conversation while
heavier work is delayed. Reliability is measured by durable records,
repeatable status, bounded retries, and visible failures. Maintainability is
measured by the ability to replace models, internal actions, workflows, and interfaces
without rewriting the kernel. Auditability is measured by the ability to explain
what data, action, policy, and verification path produced an outcome.

\section{Risk And Policy Boundaries}

\Madre treats prompt injection, data leakage, excessive agency, unsafe action
execution, poisoned knowledge, provider dependency, remote retention, unbounded
consumption, credential exposure, and rollback gaps as architectural risks. The
runtime must assume that model output can be persuasive, wrong, incomplete,
unsafe, or maliciously influenced by context.

Risk control is expressed through policy boundaries. Data classification governs
what may be retrieved, stored, disclosed, or sent to a model. Autonomy level
governs whether a result may remain a draft, update local state, invoke an internal action,
contact an external service, or require stronger authorization. Recovery expectation
governs whether the action must be reversible, snapshot-backed, compensatable,
or blocked.

\madreChapter{Runtime Governance}
\section{Runtime Authority Rule}

Runtime authority belongs to the \Madre kernel and its policy boundaries. User turns, source text, model output, action results, local inspection, and candidate material can inform decisions, but they cannot authorize their own authority, promote themselves, mutate protected state, or widen permissions. Bounded internal action use is policy-gated, local access is read-first where it inspects state, and memory/knowledge/learning changes remain inactive candidates unless promoted by authority.

\madreChapter{Governance, Deployment, And Limits}

\section{Secure Engineering Principles}

\Madre requires secure defaults for local credentials, dependency selection,
governed external boundaries, secrets handling, reviewable release records, and
vulnerability response. Security evidence must cover both conventional software
risks and model-mediated risks such as prompt injection, poisoned retrieval,
unsafe output handling, excessive agency, and remote disclosure.

\section{Open-Source Governance}

\Madre is released under \texttt{\license}. The open-source governance model
must preserve license clarity, dependency review, contribution rules, security
disclosure, release integrity, and community responsibility. Openness is part
of the project value only when users and developers can inspect, adapt, and
verify the runtime without surrendering private data to a platform owner.

\section{Deployment And Operational Assumptions}

The baseline deployment target is an ordinary local device with local storage,
local configuration, optional local model execution, and optional remote
integrations governed by policy. The operational profile includes storage paths,
model setup assumptions, backup and restore, migration, diagnostics, log
retention, offline behavior, and upgrade or rollback expectations.

Deployment assumptions remain conceptual at this level of specification. The
product architecture defines authority, locality, inspection, recovery, and
model-runtime and action boundaries without reducing deployment to a single operational
shape.

\section{Risks, Limitations, And Excluded Functions}

\Madre limits active executable behavior by authority class. Workflow authoring,
self-learning, speech interaction, graphical product surfaces, distributed
inference, organization-scale deployment, and model fine-tuning remain governed
by the same local-first, policy-governed, model-agnostic, auditable, and
recoverable runtime boundary as the rest of the product.

\madreChapter{Glossary}

\begin{tabularx}{.95\linewidth}{@{}lX@{}}
	\toprule
	\textbf{Term} & \textbf{Definition} \\
	\midrule
	\textbf{Internal action} & A typed runtime-defined executable function available to modules through the Internal Action Registry. An internal action declares inputs, outputs, side-effect class, policy requirements, audit evidence, and recovery expectations. It is not an exposed network integration surface. \N
	\textbf{Action contract} & The declaration that defines an internal action's input schema, output schema, side effects, authorization requirements, audit obligations, and recovery expectations. \N
	\textbf{Model invocation action} & A specialized internal action that invokes a local or policy-authorized model runtime. It receives governed context, model strategy, action policy, decoding configuration, and returns generated candidate material plus inference evidence. \N
	\textbf{Complex Agent} & A module-local runtime actor that may coordinate foreground, reasoning, and reflection lanes. It can answer, defer, reason, verify, and propose learning candidates, but it does not gain authority beyond module and kernel policy. \N
	\textbf{Local model runtime boundary} & The controlled boundary through which \Madre reaches local or policy-authorized model execution. It includes model profiles, quantization choices, device/resource policy, session/cache policy, inference traces, and generated candidate handling. \N
	\textbf{Reasoning query} & A recorded need for delayed analysis, evidence, internal action invocation, or verification beyond the foreground answer. \N
	\textbf{Knowledge area} & A scoped collection of source-backed knowledge with provenance, sensitivity, correction, and deletion rules. \N
	\textbf{Model profile} & A declared model execution profile with purpose, limits, cost, context behavior, and policy constraints. \N
	\textbf{Read-first console} & A local inspection surface that exposes status and audit evidence without becoming a privileged mutation path. \N
	\textbf{Inactive candidate} & Proposed memory, knowledge, learning, prompt, routing, or workflow material that is inspectable but not active runtime state. \N
	\textbf{Audit event} & A durable observation that records what happened, under which context, model invocation, action invocation, policy, and verification boundary. \N
	\textbf{Recovery path} & The defined correction, rollback, deletion, compensation, or blocking behavior for an outcome or state transition. \N
	\textbf{External tool} & A non-canonical industry term for optional integrations outside the default \Madre internal-action path. External tools and services are treated as higher-risk external actions that require explicit policy, audit, and recovery boundaries. \\
	\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

boundaries, traceability, and validation criteria in this specification.
